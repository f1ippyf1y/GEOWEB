Moscow Guide: How to Explore Moscow in 3 Days

Moscow, RU12 min readPhotography: Staff

Moscow does not reveal itself on the first pass. The city works in layers — Tsarist stone beneath Soviet concrete beneath the glass towers going up right now — and three days is just enough time to read a few of them if you know where to look.

Start low. The one-story wooden houses tucked behind Chistye Prudy survive by accident more than design, wedged between courtyards that still smell like someone's grandmother's kitchen. This is the Moscow that predates the boulevards, and it disappears a little more every year.

By the second day you are ready for scale — the Stalin-era ministries on Kotelnicheskaya, the metro stations built as underground palaces for a public that had nothing else marble. Ride the circle line end to end. It costs less than a coffee and shows you more of the city's self-image than any museum will.

Night three belongs to Neighbourhood — not a single venue but a rotating cast of basements, industrial units, and afterparties that start after the trains stop. Bring cash, bring patience for the door, and don't ask too many questions about the address.
